**D-190 — One shortcut list drives the Ctrl+/ help modal and `/help`; Ctrl+Alt+C opens a new client** · Accepted (MVP-47, 2026-11-21) · **Amends D-185** (its two-entry shortcut list) · Builds on D-091, D-123, D-127, D-133

MVP-47 asked for a global shortcut handler (Ctrl/⌘+K, Ctrl+Alt+N/C/T/U/H, Ctrl+/ help, Esc), a localized help modal
that shows ⌘ on a Mac, input-focus suppression, permissions and `aria-keyshortcuts`. Most handlers already existed, each
with the dialog it opens (search D-091, case D-123, task, upload D-133, hearing D-127). → **No new global dispatcher:**
the handlers stay where their dialogs live; what was missing is added the same way — **Ctrl+Alt+C** (`KeyC`) in the
shell's quick actions, for `manage:clients`, through `useCtrlAltShortcut` (not while typing or inside a dialog), and
**Ctrl+/** (`Slash`, ⌘/ on a Mac; not with Alt/AltGr or Shift, auto-repeat, composition, typing, or over another dialog)
opening the shortcut modal mounted in the shell. **Esc** is AntD's own close on every modal and drawer. **One list**
(`features/shell/shortcuts.tsx`, `SHORTCUTS`) feeds the modal and the `/help` card, so they cannot drift; each entry
names the permission its handler checks and is hidden from users without it (a trainee sees search, upload, help and Esc). The hearing entry checks `create:session` only; the handler also needs
write access to that case, so it may do nothing on a case the user cannot edit.
Upload and hearing are labelled "(on a case page)" — they only work there. **Writing:** Ctrl/⌘ shortcuts show ⌘ on Apple
devices and Ctrl elsewhere; Ctrl+Alt shows ⌃ ⌥ on a Mac (it is Control+Option there, not ⌘); key names are not translated
and render `dir="ltr"`. **`aria-keyshortcuts`** on the search button, the quick-action menu items, the upload and
schedule-hearing buttons, and each key in the list. **Why:** a central registry would move five working, tested handlers
and their enable conditions for no user-visible gain. **Known gap:** shortcuts follow the physical US key (`event.code`),
so on layouts where "/" sits elsewhere Ctrl+/ is the key in the US "/" position — the same rule as the letter shortcuts.
